Tap a name card to open its full story

Options for a detail view on nestjourney.app/names, how it lives next to swiping, what goes in it, and the questions to settle before building.

My recommendation

A tap on the card opens a glass bottom sheet with the name's full details. It opens at about 70% height so the deck stays visible behind it, pulls up to full screen, and closes with a drag down, the ✕ or the phone's Back. The three decision buttons stay at the bottom of the sheet, so a person can decide right from the details.

  • Build the sheet's content as one shared piece, so the public per-name pages from the SEO research can reuse it later with no second design.
  • Make the swipe card shorter at the same time: name, niqqud, one line of meaning and the two number tiles. The long parts (verse, people, full chart) move into the sheet. This also fixes a hidden problem today, below.
  • Saved names on the Lists screen open the same sheet, so the details are there when partners talk it over.

What the code and data tell us

A tap is free to use

The swipe only starts after the finger moves 8 px (gesture.ts). A press that lifts without moving does nothing today, so "tap opens details" can't be confused with a swipe. I'd add a small allowance (under 10 px, under 400 ms) so a shaky tap still counts.

Long cards lose swipe-up on phones

When a card's content is taller than the card, it scrolls inside itself and the "אהבתי מאוד" swipe-up is turned off for touch. Moving the long sections into the sheet keeps every card short, so swipe-up works on all of them.

The content is thin for now

Of the 1,295 cards, intros average about 30 characters, 425 have a checked Bible verse, and only 5 have known people (the Wikidata fetch hasn't run yet). The sheet's real value on day one is the numbers. It gets richer as content arrives.

Numbers we can show honestly

Births per year 2000–2024, all-time boys and girls, and the communities a name is common in. The boys/girls split exists only as an all-time total, not per year, so the sheet can't show "more girls lately" without new data.

Sheets already live in the URL

Filters, about and report already open as ?sheet=…, and Back closes them. The detail sheet fits that pattern as ?sheet=name&name=אריאל, which also gives each name a link that can be shared later.

Overlap with the design thread

The swipe-screen declutter (no filter chips, thinner card edges, small undo and "i" buttons) is now merged to main. The detail view will be built on top of that version.

Decisions

Kiril chose the sheet. The rest were decided on 6 Nov with the product-taste and strategy red-team checks, against the product review's "three things to get right": the card is true, the match moment, helping people decide.

  1. How it opens: the bottom sheet (A).Decided by Kiril.
  2. Partner's choice: shown only as a match, when both of you saved the name.Changed from my first pick. Showing a partner's lone save or skip, even after you decide, breaks the promise that only mutual saves are ever seen. A match badge keeps that promise and adds a small match moment inside the sheet.
  3. Similar names: every form, each marked boy, girl or for everyone.Only names that have a card in the deck, and the section hides when the list is empty. The list is checked before it ships, like the rest of the card.
  4. Shorter swipe card: yes.Swiping stays fast and swipe-up works on every card. Watch: if fewer than 1 in 10 people ever open the sheet, the chips on the card need to hint more clearly.
  5. Deciding in the sheet: yes, with the same three buttons.Reading the details is when people are most ready to decide, so the choice should be right there.
  6. Lists screen opens the same sheet: yes.Saved names are where people narrow down, which is the weakest part today.
  7. "Common among": left out of the sheet for now.Changed from my first pick. A line like "common among Muslims" on a name can read as labelling people and is easy to screenshot out of context. People who want this already have the origin filter. It can come back later in carefully reviewed wording.
